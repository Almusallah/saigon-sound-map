# Listening journey — revision, 25 September 2026

A 540-second score replaces the permanently foregrounded voice. Arrival (75s), Encounter (105s), Pulse (90s), Open space (120s), Afterimage (60s), Return (90s). Each movement sets voice, places, percussion and bass presence through separate gain stages with 24-second audio-clock ramps. Voices reach zero in Open space/Afterimage when spatial recordings are available. A voice-only filtered room retains a quiet motif. Afterimage retires the second spatial layer and silences rhythm/bass; it is sparse, not guaranteed absolute silence.

Source choice prefers actual archive categories associated with each movement, proximity, and less recently used recordings. Return preferentially recalls the first spatial source when eligible; it remains inside the room filter. Labels describe compositional intent, not a verified semantic analysis of every file. The score repeats; sources and geography vary. Places default to 55% because they now carry the journey, with a true zero control. Effects and optional synth remain off by default. No continuous generated drone has been added.

Auto journey off holds score time and position. Next movement can advance that held score manually. Audio pause freezes score time; resume retains it. Geographic passage uses audio time too, pauses for direct dragging and stays at its destination until the next movement. Reduced motion affects the waveform display, not the musical structure. Delayed JS ticks select the current movement directly without replaying a backlog; gain ramps use the audio clock. This is not a sample-accurate scheduler for future movement boundaries in a fully suspended browser: after throttling, the next available tick reconciles the score.

Previously pending changes are included: percussion +6 dB and bass +9.5 dB relative to their prior buses; 32-second eased equal-power voice replacement with protection against stacked manual replacements. Master remains at the deployed 1.8 gain. Solo voice temporarily bypasses score attenuation and silences accompaniment/effects; zero sliders remain zero. Existing effects can have a short decay tail.

Validation: 32 Sound Map tests, plus 6 Sampler regression tests passed. Tests cover movement order over a full cycle, voiceless/sparse sections, recall, manual/held/paused behavior, zero gains, replacement curves and archive failure cases. Browser verification uses real archive data via a GET-only local relay because direct B2 requests fail in the local browser environment. Browser playback/control checks are technical verification, not a claim of human listening or an offline PCM loudness measurement. Yuri should judge immersion and balance in the local preview before publication.

The interface now foregrounds the movement and live SVG amplitude ribbons, with the mix and atlas collapsed by default and compact playback controls. Mobile width 390px checked without horizontal overflow. Preview and production use the same client; local preview relays audio only for testing. No Sampler release is included. Deployment status and verified commit are recorded in the shared workspace coordination/CURRENT.md.

## Local correction — 12 October 2026

Open space and Afterimage now reconcile the voice fallback when spatial sources appear or disappear. The voice fades toward 0.25 when the final spatial source is lost, and back toward the movement’s target when a decoded source starts. Only a changed voice target triggers the 24-second ramp; ordinary layer replacements and outgoing source endings do not restart it or the other score fades. Solo voice, mute and user zero controls remain authoritative.

All 43 Sound Map tests pass, including six new lifecycle regressions covering recovery/loss, source replacement, pending fades, solo/zero/mute, delayed decoding across pause and a movement change, and failed recovery during sparse-layer retirement. The original recovery reproducer now reaches the expected voice target of zero. These are mocked audio-graph tests, not perceptual listening or hardware Safari verification. This correction is included in the October release; deployment evidence is recorded in coordination/CURRENT.md in the shared workspace.

## Scene, detail, return — 12 October 2026

On Yuri’s instruction, the score now uses a concrete relationship between opening scene and returning detail. For the first 45 score seconds automatic spatial rotation leaves the initial field recording alone; the voice and controls remain available. This is a broad excerpt of up to 65 seconds with a softened loop seam, not a claim to play every source file in full. Explicit changes still work.

The first eligible bed retains one short PCM excerpt (up to 14 source seconds, normally a 12.2-second loop after seam preparation, starting at 3 seconds or duration/5 for shorter files). Encounter installs that exact buffer as the principal field layer. Other spatial layers fall toward 28% of their relative gain over a four-second time constant; the voice score falls to 0.35, with quieter percussion and bass. This makes room through subtraction. The fragment is replaced in Pulse when another source is ready, remains absent through the spatial movements in normal operation, and Return reuses the identical buffer even if the original full-file cache has been evicted. Only one short excerpt is retained per session. No additional continuously sounding source or effect was introduced.

Late loads cannot overwrite a newer movement. A failed replacement keeps the existing source until another is ready, so absence of the excerpt is not guaranteed during network failure. Manual Change surroundings suppresses automatic detail recall for that movement; future Return can still recall it. Solo voice and zero controls remain authoritative. Source membership is checked against the current filtered corpus before recalling it.

The atlas’s virtual point of attention now heads toward the playing principal field recording (or another playing source if unavailable), rather than an independently chosen recording. This is not a reconstructed GPS journey. The source list identifies excerpt duration, offset and seam treatment and links to the original archive recording.

Validation: 49 Sound Map tests pass, including six additional scene/detail regressions for timing, exact-buffer recall, cache eviction, source continuity, stale loads, manual choice, room membership, source-linked destinations, Solo and zero controls. The existing full-cycle score and Open space regressions also pass. Selection of the excerpt is deterministic, not a human-curated semantic or transient selection. Recognizability, masking, emotional effect and listening quality require listening with Yuri/Afra; these tests do not establish them.

CASC (2022) remains the project’s own genealogy of field listening, collaboration, composition and public return. Bell and colleagues provide a way to articulate intention, choice and consequence; this implementation is our experiment, not a claim to implement their work or to prove immersion. Further curated scenes, archive-derived responsive rhythms and qualitative A/B listening remain future work.
